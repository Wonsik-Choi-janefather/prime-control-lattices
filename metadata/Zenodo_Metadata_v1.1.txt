Description / Abstract:
We introduce a self-contained mathematical toy model in which a finite set of prime numbers indexes coordinate directions of a multiplicative state space. The underlying group is a free abelian lattice, but the group alone does not canonically select the prime basis: unimodular changes of basis generate equivalent multiplicative coordinate systems. We therefore distinguish the nonnegative-exponent multiplicative monoid. Its atoms are exactly the selected primes, and its automorphism group reduces to permutations of those atoms, giving a precise prime-selection principle once an oriented factorization monoid is part of the structure. Bilateral prime shifts are symmetrized to define inversion-preserving control operators. Their product obeys an exact product-ratio mixing identity, while an m-prime product generates 2^(m-1) inversion-identified channels: one forward compositional code channel and 2^(m-1)-1 mixed-sign beat channels. A logarithmic position observable converts multiplicative coordinates into additive scale, and a quadratic logarithmic drift gives an exact bare splitting Omega log p log q between product and ratio states. For finite hard-wall truncations we prove full unitary controllability inside each inversion-parity sector under independently addressable prime-axis controls. Inversion symmetrization also erases orientation, so a physical realization would need an additional preparation or positive-monoid structure to identify which direction is primitive-forward. The construction is a mathematical control architecture, not a claim that physical nature is prime-coded.
